{"summary": "Gradient descent minimises loss L(w) by moving parameters against the gradient: w_new = w_old - alpha * dL/dw, alpha being the learning rate. Too small a rate converges slowly; too large overshoots and diverges. Batch gradient descent uses the whole training set: stable but slow on large data. Stochastic gradient descent uses one example per update: fast and noisy, helping escape shallow local minima. Mini-batch uses small batches, the usual choice. For a convex loss such as mean squared error, a suitable rate reaches the global minimum. Feature scaling speeds convergence; an epoch is one full pass through the training data."}
